CHGHealthcare

CHG Healthcare is the largest provider of locum tenens (temporary physician) staffing in the United States and one of the country's largest privately held healthcare staffing companies. Founded in Salt Lake City in 1979, it operates a family of specialized staffing brands that place physicians, advanced practitioners, allied health professionals, and nurses on temporary and permanent assignments nationwide.

The company is known as much for its workplace culture as its market position — it has appeared on Fortune's 100 Best Companies to Work For list for over a decade, ranking #40 in 2026. Its "Putting People First" philosophy shapes both its employer brand and its go-to-market model.

CHG is now undertaking a significant modernization of its sales organization — the reason for this engagement — moving toward a clearer role architecture, harmonized pay, and individual quotas across its 7 US divisions.

Business model & how they make money
What they do
  • Locum tenens — placing physicians & advanced practitioners in temporary assignments (the core, highest-share business).
  • Permanent placement — direct-hire physician and allied recruiting.
  • Allied health & nursing — therapists, technologists, and travel nurses.
  • Technology & services — credentialing (Modio), VMS (Locumsmart), recruiting media (CareerMD).
Commercial engine
  • A large, transactional sales force of client-facing reps (sell to hospitals/health systems) and provider-facing reps/recruiters (source and place clinicians) — a two-sided marketplace.
  • Revenue is driven by filled days / placements; margin sits between the bill rate to the client and pay rate to the provider.
  • Historically a commission-heavy, non-quota comp model — the very thing this engagement is modernizing.
Why this engagement — what CHG told us
Competitive pressure

The locum market is more saturated than ever; clients of all sizes are increasingly working around CHG, creating urgency to act.

Pay & role misalignment

Similar roles are paid differently and job definitions are inconsistent, creating internal equity gaps as people move across the evolving model.

Client-model strain

Division sellers are chasing jobs already sold by the enterprise client team instead of growing new and existing accounts.

Organizational readiness

After nearly a decade on the current model, leadership agrees the risk of inaction now outweighs the risk of change.

The engagement

Designing a Modern Sales Structure & Compensation Program to Accelerate Integration and Growth

A ~10-week program to clarify CHG's sales role architecture, validate market competitiveness of pay, and build the individual-accountability (quota) framework needed to sustain growth in a rapidly evolving locum tenens market — targeting an FY27 go-live for quota-based roles.

Where CHG sits
Read on the landscape

CHG is the #2 healthcare staffing company in the US overall, trailing only Aya Healthcare — but Aya's lead is built on travel nursing, not physicians. In locum tenens, CHG's core business, CHG is #1 with roughly a third of the market through CompHealth, Weatherby, and Global Medical Staffing.

The real head-to-head pressure — and the "clients working around us" the scoping brief describes — comes from three directions: Jackson Healthcare's locum brands (LocumTenens.com, Jackson & Coker), the only large public pure-play in AMN Healthcare, and a pack of fast-growing single-brand specialists (Medicus, Hayes, Barton) winning on speed and service. Consolidation is live too — Aya's $615M bid for Cross Country was recently terminated under FTC scrutiny.
